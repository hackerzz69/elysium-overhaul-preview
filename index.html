THE LORE

SONGS OF THE SHATTERING

Every realm has a beginning. Ours began with an ending.

Chapter I

The Shattering

We remember the sky breaking before we remember anything else. The gods had warred above us for a thousand years, and on the last night their weapons fell like burning rain. Mountains folded. Seas rose screaming. The old world did not end with a whisper — it ended with a wound that never closed.

From that wound bled the Aether, raw magic the gods had hoarded since creation. It pooled in the cracks of the world and learned to dream. And in those dreams it called to us — the survivors, the stubborn, the ones who refused to kneel even at the end of everything.

Chapter II

The Exile

We fled into the dark places — the deep woods, the high ice, the drowned coasts — and learned to live on what the broken world gave us. Woodcutters became wardens. Miners became war-priests of the deep stone. Fishers read the tides like scripture. We were exiles, yes, but we were alive, and the Aether sang louder with every passing year.

Then the Wastes began to move. Something old crawled out of the wound at the world’s heart, wearing the shape of our dead gods. The Ember King rose with an army of ash, and the scattered tribes knew the truth at last: we could run no longer. We had to become one people — or become nothing at all.

Chapter III

The Return

So we marched — woodcutters and wardens, miners and mages, fishers and fighters — back toward the light we had fled. We raised the Citadel stone by stone above the scar, and its spires drink the Aether now instead of fearing it. This is Elysium: not the paradise the old songs promised, but the one we built with our own hands.

The Ember King still waits in the Wastes, and the wound still bleeds. But look at us — we are the children of the Shattering, and we do not kneel. Take up your axe, your pick, your rod, your blade. The realm is calling, and it is calling your name.
